What is GLP-1?

Glucagon-like peptide-1 is a natural incretin hormone produced in your gut. It signals your brain to reduce appetite, slows stomach emptying, and regulates insulin secretion — your body's built-in weight management system.

How Semaglutide Works

Semaglutide mimics natural GLP-1 with a longer half-life. A weekly injection suppresses appetite, reduces caloric intake by ~30%, and delivers sustained weight loss of 15-17% body weight over 68 weeks.

Science

Understanding GLP-1: Your Body's Natural Weight Regulator

Glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) is an incretin hormone naturally produced by L-cells in your small intestine after eating. It plays a crucial role in glucose metabolism and appetite regulation.

When you eat, GLP-1 is released and acts on multiple organ systems: it stimulates insulin secretion from the pancreas, suppresses glucagon release, slows gastric emptying, and — critically — signals the hypothalamus in your brain to reduce appetite.

In people with obesity, this natural GLP-1 response is often blunted. Semaglutide, a GLP-1 receptor agonist, mimics and amplifies this natural hormone's effects with a weekly injection that maintains therapeutic levels throughout the week.

The result? Patients experience significantly reduced hunger, eat fewer calories naturally (studies show ~30% reduction in caloric intake), and achieve sustained weight loss that was previously only possible with bariatric surgery.

Safety

GLP-1 Side Effects: What to Expect & How to Manage

Like all medications, GLP-1 receptor agonists can cause side effects — most are mild, gastrointestinal, and resolve within 2-4 weeks as your body adjusts.

Common (10-20% of patients): Nausea (most common, usually mild), diarrhea, constipation, abdominal discomfort, decreased appetite. These typically peak during dose escalation and diminish at stable doses.

Less Common: Headache, dizziness, fatigue, injection site reactions. Rare but Serious: Pancreatitis (stop medication, seek emergency care), gallbladder issues, hypoglycemia (especially with insulin/sulfonylureas).

Management Tips: Eat smaller, more frequent meals. Avoid high-fat and spicy foods initially. Stay hydrated. Take medication in the evening. Report persistent symptoms to your doctor — dose adjustment often resolves issues.

Contraindications: Personal/family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC), Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia type 2 (MEN2), pregnancy, severe gastroparesis.

Medical

Why Medical Supervision Matters in Weight Loss

The rise of GLP-1 medications has led to a concerning trend: people obtaining these prescription drugs without proper medical supervision. This is dangerous and potentially life-threatening.

Why you need a doctor: Semaglutide requires careful dose titration (starting at 0.25mg and gradually increasing). Certain conditions like MTC, pancreatitis, and severe kidney disease are absolute contraindications. Drug interactions must be evaluated. Regular monitoring of blood glucose, kidney function, and thyroid markers is essential.
